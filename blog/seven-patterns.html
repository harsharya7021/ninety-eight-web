01 The journal Filed 09.09.2026 — about six minutes

Seven patterns from thirty-five cases

Ninety-Eight 09.09.2026 Creator marketing 2020–2023 · 70+ brands

Patterns observed, not principles claimed. Patterns are evidence; principles are marketing. What follows is the honest version of what thirty-five documented cases actually showed us — each one filed, each one openable, and each one still sitting in the archive if you want to check the arithmetic.

Six years in, the temptation is to publish a philosophy. A philosophy is cheap: it costs one afternoon and it can never be wrong. So instead, here is a list of things that kept happening. Some of them are obvious in hindsight. One of them cost us a client’s first month to learn.

01 · The constellation

A stack of creators across audience classes, landing inside a window tight enough that the city reads it as an arrival rather than a schedule. This is the single most repeatable thing we do. Slay Coffee ran fifty-eight creators. One8 Commune ran thirty-six across ninety-eight story frames — yes, ninety-eight; the coincidence wasn’t lost on us. Sky Lantern, Farzi Cafe and Deja Brew all followed the same shape.

The mechanism isn’t volume. It’s simultaneity. Twenty posts across three months is a content plan. Twenty posts across three weeks is a rumour.

02 · The single-creator anchor

The opposite move, and sometimes the right one: one verified voice whose feed already lives in the brand’s grammar. Bayroute and Ophelia were both carried this way. When the brand is precise and the audience is narrow, a constellation dilutes it. One person who genuinely belongs there is worth more than twelve who are visiting.

03 · The daypart play

Sell the hour nobody else is selling. Khubani built an afternoon — manicures while you eat, one to six — in a market where every restaurant fights over dinner. The Weekend did the same with Rock ’n’ Brunch. There is a whole piece about this one; it is the next in this journal.

04 · One creator per audience class

Founder, fashion-travel, couples-food, fashion-digital. Never five from the same shelf. Five creators with the same audience is one creator, bought five times — and the reach number that comes back will look impressive and mean almost nothing, because the overlap is invisible in the arithmetic and total in the room.

Five from one shelf is one creator, bought five times.

05 · One retainer, many voices

A shared, verified creator pool across sister brands. Yum Yum Cha Group is the reference: four brands — Yum Yum Cha, Tablespoon, Pot Pot, Wheaty — on one retainer, with a distinct voice per brand and one pool underneath. A group does not need three agencies. It needs one playbook and four tones of voice. That year ended with three awards.

06 · The founder cameo

A credibility lift that no amount of reach substitutes for. When Zorawar Kalra appears in the supporting roster of a Farzi Cafe activation, or a founder-class creator turns up at Khubani, the whole stack is re-read as endorsement rather than placement. It is rare, it cannot be bought, and it is worth structuring the calendar around when it is available.

07 · Three tiers, not one headline

The one that cost us to learn. Highlighted, confirmed, pending — counted separately instead of inflated together. Andrea’s Eatery is the reference case. It is very easy to hand a client a single number that includes creators who said yes, creators who posted, and creators whose stories are still live in a highlight, and it is very difficult to explain a month later why the number moved. Count the three tiers apart. The total is smaller and it survives the second meeting.

The thing underneath all seven

None of these are strategies. They are shapes that kept recurring once we had enough cases to see across them — which is the only reason to publish thirty-five of them in the first place. Where we do not have a case, we say so: the performance room on the marketing page carries no figure at all, because the work is live and the write-up isn’t written.

That is the whole editorial policy. Fewer claims, larger whitespace, real artifacts only.
