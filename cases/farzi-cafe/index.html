01 The Snapshot

Farzi Cafe is a Massive Restaurants concept — contemporary Indian, multi-city, already a known name. The brief to 98 was specific: re-engage Delhi's food creator community after a pandemic-era visibility dip. The KPI wasn't 'launch the brand' — Farzi was already known. It was 'reaffirm the city's relationship with the venue'.

98 anchored the campaign on Amar Sirohi (@foodie_incarnate, 2.35M verified public figure) — India's most-followed food creator. Syma Verma (766K verified reel creator), Saanya Khatter (532K verified public figure), Mehak Dhawan (@foodiesince96, 338K verified), and Harteerath Singh (297K verified) backed him up. Founder Zorawar Kalra appeared in the supporting roster — a founder-cameo pattern 98 has reused successfully on Poetry.

02 The Challenge

Re-engagement campaigns are different from launch campaigns. The brand is not new — diners have priors, both positive and dormant. The job is to remind, not to introduce. That requires creators whose audiences will read 'oh, I forgot how good that is' — not 'oh, what's that?'.

03 How It Was Run

98 ran the Farzi Cafe · Massive Restaurants launch as a four-stage operation: brief and concept, creator match, visit choreography, and asset handover.

04 What Delivered

The Farzi Cafe · Massive Restaurants campaign produced four distinct asset classes — built to live beyond the launch window and re-run on the brand's owned channels.

05 98 · The Capability

Farzi Cafe is 98's case for re-engagement campaigns. The mega-anchor (Amar Sirohi 2.35M) carries the message; the founder cameo carries the credibility; the verified density signals institutional weight. Same playbook applies to any brand that's gone quiet and needs to come back loud.
